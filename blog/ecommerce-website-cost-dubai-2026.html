How Much Does an eCommerce Website Cost in Dubai in 2026?

Real AED price bands for building an online store in Dubai, what actually drives the number, and the running costs most quotes leave out.

Published 24 Jul 2026 8 min read

The honest answer is that an eCommerce website in Dubai can cost anywhere from AED 8,000 to AED 150,000 or more in 2026. The range is that wide because the word "website" hides very different builds, from a templated Shopify store to a fully custom platform. What matters is matching the spend to the stage your business is actually at.

If you are researching prices before you commit, this guide breaks down the real bands by platform, explains what pushes a project up or down, and covers the ongoing costs that decide whether your store is affordable after launch, not just on day one.

What actually determines the cost

Two stores can differ by ten times in price for reasons that have nothing to do with how they look. The cost is driven by:

  • Platform choice: Shopify, WooCommerce, or a custom build.
  • Catalogue size and complexity: 20 products versus 5,000, with variants, bundles, and multi-currency.
  • Integrations: payment gateways, logistics and courier APIs, accounting, VAT, and marketplace sync.
  • Design depth: a clean theme versus a bespoke brand experience.
  • Who builds it: a freelancer, an agency, or a do-it-yourself setup.

2026 price bands in Dubai

These are realistic build ranges for the UAE market in 2026. They cover the initial build, not the monthly running costs further down.

Build typeTypical range (AED)Best for
Basic Shopify store8,000 to 30,000First-time sellers, fast launch
WooCommerce store12,000 to 35,000Content-heavy or WordPress-based brands
Custom eCommerce platform45,000 to 120,000+Unique workflows, scale, marketplaces
Enterprise or multi-vendor150,000+Marketplaces, large catalogues, heavy traffic

Most new UAE brands do not need a custom platform on day one. A well-built Shopify or WooCommerce store handles the first stage of growth comfortably, and you move to custom only when a real constraint forces it.

The running costs nobody quotes

The build price is only half the picture. Budget for the monthly reality:

  • Platform and hosting: Shopify subscriptions or WooCommerce hosting and maintenance.
  • Payment gateway fees: a percentage on every transaction through providers such as Network International, Stripe, Telr, or PayTabs.
  • Apps and plugins: reviews, subscriptions, shipping rules, and analytics add up quickly.
  • Marketing: the biggest ongoing line, because a store with no traffic makes no sales.

A store that is cheap to build but expensive to run is a false saving. We price builds with the twelve-month cost in view, not just the launch invoice. You can see how we think about pricing on our pricing page.

Freelancer versus agency

A freelancer is usually cheaper up front and can be the right call for a simple store. The trade-off is coordination: you often end up managing the designer, the developer, the payment setup, and the marketing separately. An agency costs more but removes that overhead and stays accountable for the result after launch. Which is right depends on how much of the project you want to run yourself.

How AI is changing the numbers

AI has quietly lowered parts of the cost. Product descriptions, category copy, and image tidying that used to take days can now be produced in hours, which trims content budgets on large catalogues. It does not replace the strategy, the integrations, or the conversion work, but it does mean a bigger catalogue is cheaper to launch than it was two years ago.
