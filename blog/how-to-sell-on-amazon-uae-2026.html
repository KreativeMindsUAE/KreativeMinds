How to Sell on Amazon.ae in 2026: A Guide for New and Non-Resident Sellers

What it actually takes to launch a seller account on Amazon.ae, from licensing and fulfilment to fees and sourcing, plus a realistic route for entrepreneurs outside the UAE.

Published 05 Aug 2026 9 min read

Amazon.ae is one of the fastest routes to reaching UAE shoppers, because the traffic, the trust, and the delivery network are already built. You are not paying to acquire an audience from scratch. The trade-off is that the platform sets the rules, takes a cut, and expects you to arrive with the right paperwork and a product that can compete. This guide walks through what selling on Amazon in the UAE looks like in 2026, what you need to start, and how to launch even if you do not live here.

If you are still deciding whether a marketplace is the right first move at all, it is worth reading Noon vs Amazon vs your own store alongside this, because the channel you pick shapes everything that follows.

Why Amazon.ae is worth the effort in 2026

Three things make Amazon attractive for a new UAE seller. First, intent: people on Amazon are there to buy, not to browse, so conversion tends to be higher than on social channels. Second, logistics: Fulfilment by Amazon lets you hand off storage, packing, delivery, and returns, which removes the single hardest part of running an online store in a new market. Third, credibility: a listing with reviews and Prime delivery carries instant trust that a brand new independent store has to earn slowly.

The cost of that leverage is real competition and thin control. You compete on the same page as everyone else, the platform owns the customer relationship, and your margin has to survive Amazon's fees. That is the deal, and it is a good one for the right products.

What you need to open a seller account

To sell on Amazon.ae as a UAE-based business in 2026, you generally need the following in place:

  • A valid trade licence. A mainland or free zone licence with an activity that covers trading or eCommerce. This is the foundation everything else hangs off.
  • A UAE bank account. Amazon pays out to a local business account, and you will need it for VAT and supplier payments too.
  • VAT registration where it applies. If your taxable turnover crosses the mandatory threshold, you register for VAT and account for it on your sales. Many sellers register voluntarily earlier to reclaim input VAT. We cover the mechanics in UAE e-invoicing and VAT in 2026.
  • Emirates ID and passport of the account owner or manager, plus a contactable UAE phone number and address.
  • Product identifiers. Most categories expect a barcode such as a GTIN or EAN, unless you qualify for brand-owner exemptions through Brand Registry.

None of this is exotic if you already run a UAE company. The friction is almost always in the sequencing: getting the licence activity right, the bank account open, and VAT sorted before you try to verify the account, so Amazon's checks pass on the first attempt instead of the third.

How to sell on Amazon.ae as a non-resident (Pakistan, Bangladesh, India, South Asia)

This is the question we get most from entrepreneurs in Pakistan, Bangladesh, India, and across South Asia: can you sell on Amazon.ae without living in the UAE? Yes. You do not need a UAE residence visa to open a seller account. What you actually need is a legitimate UAE business presence and the ability to satisfy Amazon's verification, and both can be arranged from your home country.

The part that surprises most first-time sellers is how light the structure can be. In practice, a UAE free zone company is enough to register as a seller on both Amazon.ae and Noon. A free zone licence gives you the trading entity, the documents, and the standing that the marketplaces expect, without the cost and commitment of a mainland setup or a residence visa you do not need. You set up the free zone company, arrange the banking and tax registration that go with it, and then build the seller account on that foundation so it verifies cleanly the first time.

To be clear on what a non-resident route looks like in 2026:

  • No UAE visa required to open and run the seller account.
  • A free zone company is sufficient for a seller account on both Amazon.ae and Noon.
  • You can operate from Pakistan, Bangladesh, India, or elsewhere in South Asia, provided the entity, banking, and verification are set up correctly.
  • FBA does the heavy lifting, so you do not need to be physically present in the UAE to store, pack, and deliver orders.

FBA or FBM: choosing how you fulfil

Amazon gives you two ways to get orders to customers, and the choice shapes your costs and your workload.

Fulfilment by Amazon (FBA) means you ship stock into Amazon's warehouses and they handle storage, packing, delivery, and returns. You get Prime eligibility, faster delivery badges, and far less operational load. In exchange you pay storage and fulfilment fees, and you carry the risk of stock sitting in a warehouse if it does not sell.

Fulfilment by Merchant (FBM) means you hold and ship your own stock. You keep more control and avoid FBA storage fees, but you are responsible for hitting delivery speed and service targets yourself, which is hard to do well from day one in a new market.

For most new sellers with fast-moving, compact products, FBA is the sensible starting point because it removes the logistics problem you are least equipped to solve early. Bulky, slow, or high-variation ranges often lean FBM.

Sourcing products without drowning in imports

Your first sourcing decision is quietly one of the riskiest. Import a container of the wrong product and you have tied up cash and warehouse space in stock that will not move. Two broad routes exist: import from overseas suppliers, which usually means better unit costs but longer lead times, customs paperwork, and larger minimum orders, or source locally inside the UAE, which costs a little more per unit but lets you test demand fast without shipping and clearing goods yourself.

Listings that actually convert

A great product with a weak listing still loses. On Amazon the listing is your entire storefront, so it has to earn the click and the sale in seconds. The pieces that matter most are a clear, keyword-aware title, clean high-resolution images that follow Amazon's rules, bullet points that answer the buyer's real questions, and a description that removes doubt. Reviews then compound everything: the first genuine reviews are the hardest and the most valuable, so getting early orders right matters more than almost anything else.

This is also where AI has changed the workload. Drafting and optimising listing content at scale is far faster than it was, as long as a human keeps it accurate and on-brand. We wrote about where that helps and where it backfires in AI-powered eCommerce.

Understanding the fees before you price

Amazon's economics only work if you price with the fees baked in from the start. Expect a mix of the following: a referral fee taken as a percentage of each sale that varies by category, FBA fulfilment and storage fees if you use it, a subscription cost for a professional selling plan, and the usual overheads of VAT, returns, and the cost of goods. The mistake new sellers make is pricing against the shelf price they see and only discovering the squeeze after the fees land. Build a simple margin sheet before you list, and treat any product that only works at full price with zero promotions as a red flag.

Common mistakes that stall new UAE sellers

  • Wrong licence activity. A licence that does not clearly cover trading or eCommerce causes verification headaches later.
  • Pricing without the fee stack. Margins that look healthy collapse once referral and FBA fees apply.
  • Importing before testing. Large first orders of an unproven product tie up cash you cannot get back quickly.
  • Thin listings. Poor images and vague copy kill conversion no matter how good the product is.
  • Ignoring the wider funnel. Amazon is one channel. A brand that also has its own store and audience is far more resilient, which is exactly the trade-off we unpack in Noon vs Amazon vs your own store.
