How much should a small business spend on digital marketing each month

Getting the numbers right for digital marketing can feel tricky, especially when every dirham counts.

Published June 30, 2026 5 min read

A realistic budget starts with your goals, your market and the channels that give the most bang for your buck. Below we break down how much to allocate, which low-cost platforms work best, and the simplest way to know if you are getting results.

How much should a small business spend on digital marketing each month

Most experts suggest spending between 5 % and 10 % of your projected monthly revenue on digital marketing. If you expect to make 50,000 AED per month, that works out to 2,500-5,000 AED. The range lets you adjust for growth stages: new startups often start at the lower end, while established firms can move toward the higher end.

Another practical rule is to set a fixed amount you can afford without hurting cash flow. Start with a modest figure, say 1,500 AED, track results for three months, then increase the budget if the return justifies it.

  • 5 % of revenue for brand-building activities
  • 2-3 % of revenue for direct-response campaigns
  • Reserve 10-15 % of the budget for testing new ideas

What digital marketing channels work best for a new business with a small budget

When money is tight, focus on channels that cost little to start and can be scaled. Organic social media, especially Instagram and LinkedIn, let you showcase products and engage local audiences without large ad spends.

Content marketing, short blog posts, how-to videos, and carousel posts, helps you appear in search results and builds trust over time. Pair this with a modest pay-per-click (PPC) budget on Google Search for high-intent keywords; you can control spend day by day.

Email marketing remains one of the cheapest ways to nurture leads. A simple newsletter platform costs under 100 AED per month and can turn website visitors into repeat customers.

  • Instagram and LinkedIn organic posts
  • Google Search PPC with daily caps
  • Weekly blog or video content
  • Email newsletters with automated flows

How do i measure if my marketing is actually working

Start with clear, measurable goals: website traffic, leads, sales, or app installs. Attach a metric to each goal, like 500 new visitors per month or 20 qualified leads.

Use free tools such as Google Analytics and the insights built into Facebook and Instagram. Track source/medium, conversion rate, and cost per acquisition (CPA). If CPA is lower than the profit you make on a sale, the campaign is delivering value.

Review the data weekly. Look for trends rather than single-day spikes. Adjust budgets toward the channels with the lowest CPA and highest return.

Budgeting, channel selection, and measurement are three pillars that turn a modest spend into measurable growth. With the right data, you can keep refining your approach and avoid waste.
